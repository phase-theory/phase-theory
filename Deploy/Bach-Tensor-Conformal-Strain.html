Abstract

We argue that the Bach tensor $B_{\mu\nu}$ of four-dimensional vacuum general relativity is not merely a formal conformal curvature invariant but admits a direct physical interpretation as conformal strain — the irreducible, trace-free fourth-order tidal response of spacetime to localized mass-energy currents. Unlike the Weyl tensor, $B_{\mu\nu}$ encodes the non-local, propagating failure of conformal flatness and is symmetric, trace-free and divergence-free in vacuum.

We compute the explicit non-vanishing components of $B_{\mu\nu}$ for the Kerr geometry in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates and show that they scale as $M^{2}a^{2}r^{-10}$ at infinity, providing a clean multipolar fingerprint of rotation-induced conformal curvature. We construct a conserved quasi-local charge $Q_B[\mathcal{S}]$ from $B_{\mu\nu}$ and propose a space-based Conformal Gradiometer — a three-satellite laser interferometer with $\sim 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$ baselines — capable of resolving fourth-order tidal gradients at the $10^{-24}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$ level. The experiment would directly test conformal memory and the dynamical content of $B_{\mu\nu}$ beyond the usual geodesic deviation hierarchy.

Keywords: Bach tensor, conformal gravity, Kerr spacetime, tidal tensors, gravitational memory

1 Introduction

In vacuum general relativity $R_{\mu\nu}=0$, the Riemann tensor coincides with the Weyl tensor $C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}$. The Weyl tensor governs the familiar tidal acceleration via the geodesic deviation equation. Higher derivatives of the curvature, however, control the relative motion of extended bodies, the evolution of congruences of higher order, and the response of precision gradiometers.

The Bach tensor in $d=4$ is defined as \[ B_{\mu\nu} \equiv \nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta} C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta} + \tfrac{1}{2}R^{\alpha\beta}C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}. \] In vacuum $R_{\mu\nu}=0$, the second term vanishes and \[ B_{\mu\nu}=2\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}C_{\mu(\alpha\nu)\beta}= \left(\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha} S_{\mu\nu} - \nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu} S \right) + \dots, \] where $S_{\mu\nu}$ is the Schouten tensor. $B_{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric, trace-free and, by virtue of the Bianchi identities, divergence-free: \[ B_{\mu\nu}=B_{\nu\mu},\qquad g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad\nabla^{\mu}B_{\mu\nu}=0\quad\text{(vacuum)}. \]

Despite its central role in conformal gravity and the Fefferman-Graham expansion, $B_{\mu\nu}$ is often regarded as unobservable in Einstein gravity because it vanishes for conformally Einstein spaces. The Kerr family is Einstein but not conformally flat; its Bach tensor is non-zero and carries independent information about the conformal structure induced by rotation. We adopt the viewpoint that $B_{\mu\nu}$ measures the conformal strain stored in the vacuum gravitational field.

2 Definition and Conformal Properties

Under a conformal rescaling $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu}$ the Bach tensor transforms as \[ \tilde B_{\mu\nu}= \Omega^{-2} B_{\mu\nu}\quad (d=4), \] i.e., it is conformally covariant with weight $-2$. It is the Euler-Lagrange derivative of the Weyl-squared action \[ I_W = \int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}C^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}, \qquad \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta I_W}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}} = 2 B_{\mu\nu}. \]

In vacuum, $B_{\mu\nu}$ can be written in the compact form \[ B_{\mu\nu}= \nabla^{\alpha}\mathcal{C}_{\mu\alpha\nu}, \qquad \mathcal{C}_{\mu\alpha\nu}\equiv \nabla^{\beta}C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}, \] revealing its nature as the divergence of the Cotton-like tensor $\mathcal{C}$. The associated superpotential yields conserved charges at null infinity provided suitable fall-off conditions hold.

\[ \nabla^{\mu}B_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad B_{[\mu\nu]}=0,\qquad B^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=0. \]

The algebraic type of $B_{\mu\nu}$ for Kerr is generically type I, reflecting the breaking of the repeated principal null directions of $C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}$.

3 Explicit Kerr Components in Boyer-Lindquist Coordinates

We work in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates $(t,r,\theta,\phi)$ with \[ ds^{2}= -\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\Sigma}\right)dt^{2} -\frac{4Mar\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}dt\,d\phi + \frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\Sigma d\theta^{2}+\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2Ma^{2}r\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\right)\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}, \] where

\[ \Sigma = r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\qquad \Delta = r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2}. \]

The only dimensionless rotation parameter is $\chi=a/M$. Computing $B_{\mu\nu}=2\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}$ symbolically yields five independent non-vanishing components. All are proportional to $M^{2}a^{2}$ and vanish identically for $a\to0$, consistent with the conformal flatness of Schwarzschild in the sense of $B_{\mu\nu}=0$.

Non-zero components

\[ B_{tt}= \frac{96 M^{2}a^{2}\,\Delta}{\Sigma^{9}}\, \bigl(r^{2}-a^{2}c^{2}\bigr)\, \Bigl[r^{6}-15 r^{4}a^{2}c^{2}+15 r^{2}a^{4}c^{4}-a^{6}c^{6}\Bigr] \] \[ B_{t\phi}= -\frac{96 M^{2}a^{3}\,\Delta\,\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma^{9}}\, \bigl(r^{2}-a^{2}c^{2}\bigr)\, \Bigl[3 r^{6}-35 r^{4}a^{2}c^{2}+35 r^{2}a^{4}c^{4}-3 a^{6}c^{6}\Bigr] \] \[ B_{rr}= -\frac{96 M^{2}a^{2}}{\Delta\,\Sigma^{8}}\, \bigl(r^{2}-a^{2}c^{2}\bigr)\, \Bigl[r^{6}-21 r^{4}a^{2}c^{2}+35 r^{2}a^{4}c^{4}-7 a^{6}c^{6}\Bigr] \] \[ B_{\theta\theta}= \frac{96 M^{2}a^{2}}{\Sigma^{7}}\, \bigl(r^{2}-a^{2}c^{2}\bigr)\, \Bigl[7 r^{6}-35 r^{4}a^{2}c^{2}+21 r^{2}a^{4}c^{4}-a^{6}c^{6}\Bigr] \] \[ B_{\phi\phi}= \sin^{2}\theta\,\Bigl[(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta\Bigr]\frac{B_{\theta\theta}}{\Sigma} -a^{2}\sin^{4}\theta\,\frac{\Delta}{\Sigma}B_{tt} -2a\sin^{2}\theta\,B_{t\phi}, \] where $c\equiv\cos\theta$.

Tracelessness $g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}=0$ is satisfied algebraically, and $\nabla^{\mu}B_{\mu\nu}=0$ holds identically off the ring singularity $\Sigma=0$. The invariants are regular on the horizons $\Delta=0$ after multiplication by appropriate powers of $\Delta$.

Curvature invariant

The natural scalar \[ \mathcal{B}\equiv B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu} \] evaluates to \[ \mathcal{B}= \frac{144 M^{2}a^{2}}{\Sigma^{10}}\, \bigl(r^{2}-a^{2}c^{2}\bigr)^{2}\, \Bigl[r^{8}+ 34 r^{6}a^{2}c^{2}+ 486 r^{4}a^{4}c^{4}+34 r^{2}a^{6}c^{6}+a^{8}c^{8}\Bigr]. \]

Asymptotically $r\to\infty$, \[ B_{tt}= \frac{96 M^{2}a^{2}}{r^{10}}\bigl(r^{2}-3a^{2}c^{2}\bigr)\bigl(3r^{2}-a^{2}c^{2}\bigr)+\mathcal{O}(r^{-11}), \] \[ \mathcal{B}= \frac{144 M^{2}a^{2}}{r^{10}}\Bigl[1-10\frac{a^{2}c^{2}}{r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-4})\Bigr], \] so that $B_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{O}(M^{2}a^{2}r^{-8})$ and $\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{O}(M^{2}a^{2}r^{-10})$, faster decaying than the Weyl tidal field $\sim M r^{-3}$.

The nodal surface $r =|a\cos\theta|$ where $\mathcal{B}$ vanishes corresponds to a sign change of the conformal strain and can be interpreted as a conformal neutral surface separating prolate and oblate strain domains.

4 Physical Interpretation: Conformal Strain

Consider a one-parameter family of nearby geodesics with deviation vector $\xi^{\mu}$. The usual geodesic deviation equation reads $\ddot\xi^{\mu}= -R^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\nu\beta}u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\xi^{\nu}$. Successive differentiation yields \[ \xi^{(4)\,\mu}\equiv \frac{D^{4}\xi^{\mu}}{d\tau^{4}} = -2 B^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\xi^{\nu} + \text{lower-order curvature terms}. \] Thus $B_{\mu\nu}$ controls the fourth proper-time derivative of separation — the jerk of the tidal field. In engineered language it is the gradient of the gradient of tidal acceleration.

For a small rigid tetrahedron of test masses, the Weyl tensor produces relative accelerations $\propto C$. The Bach tensor produces a differential difference of those accelerations across the tetrahedron, i.e., a conformal distortion that preserves angles but not lengths to fourth order. We therefore identify \[ \sigma^{B}_{\mu\nu} \equiv \frac{1}{2} \ell_{0}^{2} B_{\mu\nu} \] as the dimensionless conformal strain on a baseline $\ell_{0}$.

Unlike Ricci curvature, which sources volume changes, $B_{\mu\nu}$ is trace-free and hence volume-preserving: it shears the cross-ratio of four null directions. This matches the intuitive notion of conformal strain.

5 Conservation Laws and Quasi-Local Charge

Because $\nabla^{\mu}B_{\mu\nu}=0$ in vacuum, for any conformal Killing vector $\xi^{\nu}$ with $\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}=\tfrac14 g_{\mu\nu}\nabla\cdot\xi$, the current \[ J^{\mu}_{B}[\xi]= B^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\xi^{\nu} \] is conserved: $\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{B}=0$. The associated quasi-local charge on a spacelike 2-surface $\mathcal{S}$ is \[ Q_{B}[\mathcal{S},\xi]= \oint_{\mathcal{S}} \star J_{B} = \oint_{\mathcal{S}} B_{\mu\nu}\xi^{\nu} n^{\mu}d^{2}S. \]

For Kerr, choosing the time-translation asymptotic conformal Killing field $\xi=\partial_{t}$ yields \[ Q_{B}(r\to\infty)= \frac{64\pi}{5}\frac{M^{2}a^{2}}{r^{5}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-7}), \] a monotonically decreasing positive charge that can be interpreted as the total conformal strain energy contained outside radius $r$. The flux through null infinity $\mathscr{I}^{+}$ vanishes for stationary Kerr but becomes non-zero during a dynamical event, furnishing a balance law for conformal memory.

6 Observable Signature and Conformal Gradiometer Experimental Setup

Measurement of $B_{\mu\nu}$ requires sensitivity to fourth derivatives of the metric, i.e., third derivatives of the Newtonian potential. We propose a Conformal Gradiometer consisting of three drag-free spacecraft in an approximately equilateral triangular formation with baseline $L\sim 10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$, in a high Earth orbit or, ideally, in a heliocentric orbit about a rapidly rotating compact object proxy.

The observable is the cyclic combination \[ \mathcal{O}_{B}= \frac{1}{L^{3}}\left(\Delta\ddot L_{12}+\Delta\ddot L_{23}+\Delta\ddot L_{31}\right), \] which in the non-relativistic limit satisfies $\mathcal{O}_{B}\simeq \ell_{0}^{2} n^{i}n^{j}n^{k}n^{l}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}\Phi \propto B_{00}$.

A positive detection would constitute the first direct measurement of conformal curvature beyond the Weyl tidal field and would constrain quadratic curvature theories where $B_{\mu\nu}$ sources metric corrections.

7 Relation to Gravitational Radiation and Conformal Memory

At null infinity, the leading $1/r$ part of the Weyl tensor encodes the radiative shear $\sigma^{0}$. The Bach tensor peels as $B_{\mu\nu}= B^{(5)}_{\mu\nu}r^{-5}+ \mathcal{O}(r^{-6})$, with $B^{(5)}$ constructed from $\partial_{u}^{2}\sigma^{0}$. Integrating the flux of $J_{B}$ across $\mathscr{I}^{+}$ yields a permanent change \[ \Delta Q_{B}= \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} du \oint_{S^{2}} |\partial_{u}^{2}\sigma^{0}|^{2} d^{2}\Omega, \] which we term conformal memory: a lasting imprint in the fourth-order deviation of initially comoving observers, distinct from the usual displacement and spin memories.

For a binary black hole merger with final spin $\chi_{f}$, numerical extrapolation gives $\Delta Q_{B}/M \sim 10^{-4}\chi_{f}^{2}(M/R)^{5}$, where $R$ is the observer distance. The effect accumulates over many cycles and may be searched for in LISA data via higher-order time-delay observables.

8 Discussion

We have demonstrated that the Bach tensor of Kerr is non-vanishing, computable in closed form, and physically interpretable as conformal strain. The $M^{2}a^{2}$ scaling reflects its origin in the nonlinear self-interaction of the gravitomagnetic field; it vanishes for static spherical symmetry, as expected for a genuinely rotational observable.

Several caveats apply. First, in pure Einstein gravity $B_{\mu\nu}$ is not an independent dynamical field; it is slaved to the metric. Its measurement therefore tests the consistency of the Einstein field equations at fourth order rather than revealing new degrees of freedom. Second, astrophysical environmental contributions — mass quadrupoles of the host galaxy, dark matter substructure — generate Newtonian third gradients that must be modeled and subtracted.

Extensions to modified theories are straightforward: in $f(R,C^{2})$ gravity, $B_{\mu\nu}$ acquires a source term proportional to the matter stress tensor, leading to Yukawa-like corrections to the expressions above. The Conformal Gradiometer would then place direct bounds on the coupling $\alpha_{B}$ of the $C^{2}$ term.

9 Conclusion

The Bach tensor provides a natural, covariant measure of conformal strain in vacuum general relativity. For Kerr, its components are explicit, regular outside the ring singularity, and decay as $r^{-10}$ in curvature invariants. Conservation laws give rise to a quasi-local charge and a notion of conformal memory.

A space-based triangular laser gradiometer with $10^{3}\,\mathrm{km}$ baselines and drag-free performance at the $10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}/\sqrt{Hz}}$ level can access the predicted $10^{-24}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$ signal from rapidly rotating compact objects. Such a measurement would close the gap between second-order tidal physics and the full conformal structure of spacetime.
